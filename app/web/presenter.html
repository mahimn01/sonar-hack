Camera-free care, from the laptop already in the room.

A MacBook plays sound too high to hear and listens to the echoes with its one microphone. From them it reads hand gestures, notices when someone comes, goes or stops moving, and estimates a still person's breathing. No camera. Nothing to wear.

An ordinary laptop is already a sonar.

  • The speakers play near-inaudible sound at 18.5–21.5 kHz, the top edge of human hearing and the first range to fade with age.
  • One microphone hears the echoes off hands, bodies and a chest. Each speaker plays its own tone, which is how one microphone can tell left from right.
  • Motion shifts the echo's pitch (the Doppler effect). Breathing shifts its timing: at 20 kHz a wave is 1.7 cm long, so a 4 mm breath moves the echo by almost half a wave.
  • No new hardware. The speaker, microphone and processor are already in the room.

Sound out, echoes in, numbers out.

  1. 1EmitAn 18.5 kHz tone from the left speaker, 19.0 kHz from the right, and a 19.5–21.5 kHz chirp, looping every 20 ms
  2. 2ListenOne microphone records at 48 kHz. Nothing is stored
  3. 3CleanCut speech and room noise, then the speaker-to-mic leak and still echoes
  4. 4ReadPitch shift of the two tones for hand motion and its side. Chirp echoes by distance for presence and breathing
  5. 5DecideA small CNN names the gesture: swipe left, swipe right or push. Presence and breathing need no training

Under the hood

Signal

  • 18.5 kHz from the left speaker, 19.0 kHz from the right, a 19.5–21.5 kHz chirp, every 20 ms
  • The MacBook's own speakers and one microphone, 48 kHz; the receiver finds its own timing and re-locks by itself

Processing

  • Speech filtered out first, then the speaker-to-mic leak and still echoes
  • A 64-bin Doppler picture per tone (about ±2.3 m/s), 50 frames a second
  • Distance in 2.7 cm steps to 2.6 m. Breathing from echo phase: 1 mm of chest = 43°

Decisions

  • Gestures: a small CNN trained on this MacBook's recordings of 3 people and a simulator fitted to it
  • Left and right from the two speakers' tones, heard by one microphone
  • Presence and breathing: signal processing, no training. Everything on the laptop CPU

From one laptop to every room

  • Clinical validationBreathing rate against a respiration belt and nurse counts, across ages, chairs and beds.
  • Overnight breathingAt the bedside, lying down and under a blanket, building on contactless sleep apnea research such as UW's ApneaApp (MobiSys 2015).
  • Fall detectionA first rule is in the app, off by default: one sudden one-way movement, then stillness. Next: testing it on real falls.
  • On the caregiver's phoneToday's alerts and timeline, sent to a family member's or nurse's phone. Never footage.
  • More people, more devicesToday's model knows one MacBook and three people. Next: tablets, smart speakers and TVs already in the room.
