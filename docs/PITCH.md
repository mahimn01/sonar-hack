# Pitch scripts: 3 minutes and 90 seconds

**P** is the presenter: P talks, sits at the laptop and makes the gestures. **T** is a teammate who plays the caregiver: T stands a step back and steps in to press Acknowledge. Read the seven rules before rehearsing. They come straight from how the app behaves, and each one can sink the demo.

The live part is touch-free control, the help gesture and the "No movement" alert. Breathing is on screen as an early estimate, and nothing in either script depends on it.

One placeholder: `[RATE]` is the breathing number on the screen, if there is one. Read it, never guess. If the card shows a dash, say the dash line.

The gesture score, as one spoken sentence: "With each of our three people held out of training in turn, it recognised 133 of 206 gestures, 65 percent, and 22 of 499 other movements set it off."

## Seven rules for the live part

1. **P is one of the three people in the recordings, and gestures the way they were recorded.** Open hand, a hand-length (20 to 25 cm) from the screen. Sweep across in about half a second, then let the hand drop to your lap. Do not swipe back. A fast flick is missed: the signal follows a hand up to about 2.3 m/s.
2. **The key goes to the app in front.** Click into the slides before you swipe. Before you push, click an empty part of the dashboard, not a button: the help screen shows there, and if only one push is seen, its Space key lands on the dashboard 2.5 seconds later.
3. **Two pushes, a full second apart.** Push, bring the hand back, push again. Sooner than 1.0 s counts as the same push. Later than 2.5 s is too late. Then keep your hands down: the screen says "Help gesture seen" for 3 seconds before the alarm, and another push in that time sends it at once.
4. **For "No movement", be still for 15 seconds.** Hands down, body still, keep talking. Any gesture or body movement near the laptop starts the count again: yours, T stepping in, or a judge leaning over the table. Before that moment, do not sit still for 15 seconds, or the alert comes early.
5. **Breathing is shown, never promised.** Call it an early estimate. Read a number only if one is on the screen. While you talk, a dash is the normal answer.
6. **One miss, then the keyboard.** If a swipe is not seen, press the arrow key and keep talking. Never retry twice.
7. **One driver for the slides.** With a PDF or Keynote deck, Sonar Care presses the keys: gesture control on. With the web deck (`presenter.html`), switch Sonar Care's gesture control off in the menu bar first. The web deck changes slides on a swipe by itself, and with both on, one swipe can move two slides. The help gesture and the "No movement" alert work either way.

Which deck where:

- **At the table, 90 seconds:** a PDF or Keynote deck in its own window, and Sonar Care presses the keys. This shows the real product. Keep the web deck closed.
- **On stage, 3 minutes:** the web deck (10 slides) with Sonar Care's gesture control switched off. Slide 5 is the live slide, slide 6 is Sonar Care, and **C** opens the dashboard in a new tab.

## The 3-minute version (stage, or a long judging slot)

Setup when the clock starts: Sonar Care running. Menu bar: Gesture control off, Room monitoring on. Settings: the no-movement choice "Demo · 15 s". The web deck in front on slide 1, full screen (**F**). The chair is at the tape mark (chest 0.6 m from the screen). P stands back from the table, more than about 1.3 m from the laptop, and steps in to press →. T stands back on the other side.

Standing back matters: with "Demo · 15 s", anyone who sits still in front of the laptop for 15 seconds raises the alert, and slides 1 to 4 take a minute.

### 0:00-0:15 · Slide 1, title

**P:** "This MacBook's camera is taped over. Its speakers are playing sound too high to hear, and its one microphone is listening to the echoes. In the next two minutes it will take a call for help without being touched."

### 0:15-0:35 · Slide 2, the problem

**P** presses →.

**P:** "One in four adults over 65 falls every year. In a study of people over 90, 82 percent of falls happened when they were alone, and in most of the long waits on the floor, nobody used their call alarm. Those falls happen in bedrooms and bathrooms, where nobody wants a camera."

### 0:35-0:55 · Slide 3, the insight

**P** presses →.

**P:** "But almost every room already has a speaker and a microphone. These speakers are playing sound above 18 kilohertz, and most adults can't hear it. The microphone catches the echo. A moving hand changes its pitch. A breathing chest changes its timing."

### 0:55-1:07 · Slide 4, how it works

**P** presses →.

**P:** "The left speaker plays one tone and the right speaker another, so one microphone can tell a hand moving left from a hand moving right. A small neural network names the gesture."

### 1:07-1:25 · Slide 5, the live slide

**P** presses →, then sits down at the mark.

**P** (pointing at the presence reading): "It has noticed that someone sat down, and about how far away. Now the slide."

*P swipes right. Slide 6 comes in. Those two sentences are also the wait: the engine ignores gestures for up to 3 seconds while it thinks someone is still arriving.*

**P:** "Hands full, gloves on, or cannot reach the keyboard."

### 1:25-1:35 · Slide 6, Sonar Care

**P:** "This is Sonar Care. In any app, that swipe presses the real arrow key. It also takes a call for help."

**P** presses **C**. The dashboard opens.

### 1:35-2:00 · Dashboard: the help gesture

**P:** "Two pushes mean help."

*P pushes twice, about a second apart. The screen says "Help gesture seen" and counts down from 3.*

**P** (hands down): "Three seconds to cancel a mistake. A hand cannot cancel it. Only a person at the screen can."

*The chime plays, the laptop says "Help requested", the screen turns red. T steps in, presses Acknowledge and steps back.*

**P:** "It logged how long the answer took."

### 2:00-2:25 · Dashboard: no movement

*P sits still, hands down, and keeps talking.*

**P:** "Now I am sitting still. This card is an early breathing estimate. On one microphone it is experimental, so it shows a dash unless it is sure. And when someone is here but has not moved..."

*After 15 seconds the "No movement" banner and chime arrive.*

**P:** "...the caregiver gets this. In real use it waits a minute."

*P stands up. The alert resolves.*

**P:** "I move, and it clears by itself."

### 2:25-2:40 · Slide 7, healthcare impact

**P** goes back to the deck's tab (press **F** if it left full screen, **O** if the live panel is open), then presses → from slide 6. P stays standing.

**P:** "Why it matters. A pendant only helps if it is worn, charged and pressed. Two pushes need nothing worn, and presence needs no image. Breathing is the vital sign hospitals record least, so we show it, as an early estimate."

### 2:40-2:52 · Slide 8, under the hood

**P** presses →.

**P:** "The honest numbers. We tested on each of our three people while they were held out of training: 65 percent of gestures recognised, and 4.4 percent of other movements set it off. Three people, one laptop."

### 2:52-3:00 · Slides 9 and 10, what's next and close

**P** presses →.

**P:** "Next: more people and more devices."

**P** presses →.

**P:** "Camera-free care, from the laptop already in the room. Come and push twice at our table."

### If you're running long

Cut slide 4's lines and slide 9's line. Never cut the help gesture.

## The 90-second version (judges at the table)

Setup: Sonar Care running, the dashboard in front with no alert on it. Settings: "Slides / PDF" and the no-movement choice "Demo · 15 s". Gesture control on. A PDF or Keynote deck open in its own window. P seated at the tape mark, about 0.6 m from the laptop. T a step back. Camera taped over.

**P (0:00, pointing at the Room card):** "This MacBook's camera is taped over. Its speakers are playing sound too high to hear, and its one microphone is listening to the echoes. It knows someone is here, and about how far away."

**P (0:15, clicks into the slides):** "Touch-free control." *(Swipe right: the next slide. Swipe left: back.)* "Hands full, gloves on, or cannot reach the keyboard. It presses a real key, so it works in any app."

**P (0:35, clicks an empty part of the dashboard):** "Two pushes mean help." *(Push twice, about a second apart. "Help gesture seen", then 3 seconds.)* "Three seconds to cancel a mistake." *(Chime, the laptop says "Help requested", red screen. T steps in, presses Acknowledge, steps back.)* "The timeline shows how long the answer took."

**P (0:55, sitting still, hands down):** "Now I am sitting still. Everything that happened is in this timeline. This card is an early breathing estimate: on one microphone it is experimental, so it shows a dash unless it is sure. And when someone is here but has not moved for fifteen seconds..." *(The "No movement" banner and chime arrive.)* "...the caregiver gets this. In real use it waits a minute." *(Move a hand.)* "I move, and it clears by itself."

**P (1:20):** "No camera, nothing to wear, no new hardware. It runs on the laptop's own processor, and nothing leaves the laptop."

If a number is on the breathing card at 0:55, you may read it: "[RATE] breaths a minute, an early estimate." Never wait for one.

If a judge asks how well it works, or you have ten seconds left, give the gesture score sentence from the top of this page, then: "That is three people on one laptop."

### If you only have 60 seconds

Skip the swipe back. Say the 1:20 lines while you sit still, so the "No movement" alert arrives as you finish. If it has not come when you finish, do not wait for it.

### Optional extra: the breath-hold

Only if the breathing number has been steady in rehearsal on this MacBook. Never in the 90 seconds.

1. Settings > Room monitor: choose "Standard · 1 min", so the "No movement" alert does not come first.
2. T sits at the mark, still and silent. Wait for a breathing number, then count 5 seconds: the alert only arms once a steady rate has been on screen that long.
3. T holds their breath. The red "No breathing movement" alert comes after 10 seconds without breathing movement. T breathes, and it resolves.
4. Switch back to "Demo · 15 s".

Say: "Ten seconds without breathing is the clinical definition of an apnea. This one is experimental." If no number appears within 30 seconds, drop it and say: "It shows a dash because it is not sure. A wrong number is worse than none."

## When the live part goes wrong (say this, then act)

| What happens | Say | Do |
|---|---|---|
| A swipe is not seen | "It didn't catch that one, I'll use the keyboard." | Press the arrow key. Never retry twice. |
| A swipe goes the wrong way | "Wrong way. Misses like that are in the score we report." | Press the right arrow key. |
| One swipe moves two slides | Nothing. | Press ← once. |
| No "Help gesture seen" after two pushes | "Two pushes, a full second apart. Once more." | Push twice again, slower. If it fails again, go on to the still moment and offer a retry afterwards. |
| "Help gesture seen" after a single push | "About one single push in twelve looks like two. That is what the three seconds are for." | Press Cancel. |
| The red screen comes but no chime or voice | "The alert is on the screen." | T presses Acknowledge. Between visits, press "Play a test alert" in Settings and set the alert volume there. Do not touch the system volume. |
| No "No movement" after 20 seconds | "Something near the laptop moved, and that starts its count again." | Go on to the close. |
| "No movement" comes early | "That is the no-movement alert. I sat still for fifteen seconds." | Move a hand: it resolves. Do the planned still moment only if there is time. |
| "Sensor offline" or "Restarting the sensor" | "The sensor restarted itself. It needs a few seconds, then five more to re-learn the room." | Keep still and keep talking. If it happens twice, play the backup video (`DEMO_RUNBOOK.md`, section 5). |

## Words to use and avoid

| Avoid | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "ultrasound" for the whole signal | "sound too high to hear", "sound above what most adults can hear" | The lowest tone is 18.5 kHz, and ultrasound usually means above 20 kHz. An acoustics judge will notice. |
| "the microphones" | "its one microphone" | The MacBook gives one. Left and right come from the two speakers. |
| "works on any laptop" | "an ordinary laptop"; "trained for this one MacBook" | The gesture model only fits this MacBook. |
| "measures breathing", "breathing monitor" | "an early breathing estimate" | It is experimental on one microphone and shows a dash when it is not sure. |
| "detects falls" | "a No movement alert"; "we do not claim fall detection yet" | It has not been tested on real falls. Someone who falls out of range reads as "room empty". |
| "it phones someone", "it alerts a nurse" | "it raises Help requested on this laptop" | Today the alert stays on the laptop: red screen, notification, chime and voice. |
| "medical grade", "diagnoses" | "a prototype" | Not validated, not cleared. |
| any accuracy number except the held-out one | "65 percent of gestures, 133 of 206, on people held out of training" | Scores on people in the training set flatter the model. Never quote the 86 percent of the best-matched person alone. |
| any gesture other than the three | "swipe left, swipe right, push" | The model knows those three and nothing else. |
| "it sees" | "it senses", "it hears" | The point is that nothing sees. |
